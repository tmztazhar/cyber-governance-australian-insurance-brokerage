# Data Classification Policy

## Project: Cyber Security Governance Framework for an Australian Insurance Brokerage

### Organisation: HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd
### Document Owner : Operations Manager
### Approved By : Managing Director
### Version : 1.0
### Document Status : Portfolio Project – Fictional Business Scenario

## 1. Purpose
The purpose of this Data Classification Policy is to define how HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd classifies, handles, stores, shares, and protects information based on sensitivity and business importance.

HarbourShield handles customer personal information, insurance policy documents, claims information, financial records, business customer information, insurer communications, and internal company documents. Not all information requires the same level of protection. Data classification helps employees understand which information is low-risk, which information is sensitive, and which information requires stronger controls.

This policy supports practical data protection for a small-to-medium Australian insurance brokerage.

## 2. Policy Objectives
The objectives of this policy are to:
1. Define simple information classification levels.
2. Help employees identify sensitive information.
3. Reduce the risk of accidental disclosure or unauthorised sharing.
4. Support appropriate access control based on data sensitivity.
5. Improve handling of customer, insurance, claims, and financial information.
6. Support incident response by identifying the sensitivity of affected information.
7. Support the Information Asset Register and Cyber Risk Register.

## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, temporary workers, and authorised third parties.
- All customer, employee, supplier, insurer, policy, claims, financial, and operational information.
- All information stored, processed, transmitted, accessed, printed, shared, archived, or deleted by HarbourShield.
- Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, insurance CRM, Xero, WordPress, insurer portals, laptops, email, backups, and approved business systems.
- Information handled by outsourced IT providers and third-party suppliers on behalf of HarbourShield.

## 4. Data Classification Principles
HarbourShield’s data classification approach is based on the following principles:
| Principle | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Sensitivity-based protection | Information must be protected based on its sensitivity and business value. |
| Need to know | Access to sensitive information must be limited to users with a valid business reason. |
| Least privilage | Users should only receive the minimum access required for their role. |
| Secure handling | Sensitive information must be stored, shared, and disposed of securely. |
| Business accountability | System and information owners are responsible for classifying and protecting information. |
| Practical simplicity | Classification should be easy for employees to understand and apply. |

## 5. Classification Levels
HarbourShield uses four classification levels:
| Classification | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Information approved for public release. | Website content, published marketing material, public contact details. |
| Internal | Information intended for HarbourShield internal business use only. | Internal procedures, staff announcements, standard operating templates. |
| Confidential | Sensitive customer, business, supplier, insurer, or financial information requiring restricted access. | Customer records, policy documents, CRM records, Xero invoices, supplier contracts, internal reports. |
| Restricted | Highly sensitive information that could cause serious harm if disclosed, changed, lost, or misused. | Identity documents, breach investigation files, privileged credentials, admin passwords, legal correspondence, serious claims information. |

Where users are unsure, they should treat the information as Confidential until confirmed otherwise by the Operations Manager or relevant system owner.

## 7. Handling Requirements by Classification
| Classification | Access | Storage | Sharing | Disposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Available to the public after approval. | Approved website, public folders, marketing platforms. | May be shared externally once approved. | Dispose when no longer useful. |
| Internal | HarbourShield employees and authorised users only. | Approved internal systems such as SharePoint or OneDrive. | Internal sharing only unless approved. | Delete or archive when no longer required. |
| Confidential | Authorised users with business need only. | Approved business systems with restricted access. | Share only with authorised recipients using approved channels. | Secure deletion or controlled disposal. |
| Restricted | Strictly limited access only. | Approved restricted-access systems or protected folders. | Share only when necessary and approved. | Secure deletion, controlled disposal, or formal retention process. |

## 8. Public Information
Public information is information approved for external publication.

Examples include:
- Website content.
- Public marketing material.
- Public contact information.
- Approved service descriptions.
- Approved social media content.
- Public job advertisements.

Requirements:
- Public information must be approved before release.
- Public information must not contain customer personal information.
- Public information must not disclose internal system details, credentials, security controls, or incident information.
- Public statements on behalf of HarbourShield must only be made by authorised personnel.

## 9. Internal Information
Internal information is intended for HarbourShield employees and authorised users.

Examples include:
- Internal procedures.
- Staff announcements.
- Internal training material.
- General internal templates.
- Non-sensitive meeting notes.
- Internal project plans.

Requirements:
- Internal information must be stored in approved company systems.
- Internal information should not be shared externally unless approved.
- Internal information should not be posted publicly.
- Users should avoid storing internal information in personal accounts or unauthorised tools.

## 10. Confidential Information
Confidential information includes sensitive customer, business, insurance, financial, supplier, or employee information.

Examples include:
- Customer contact information.
- Insurance policy documents.
- Claims information.
- CRM records.
- Xero invoices and payment records.
- Supplier contracts.
- Business customer information.
- Internal management reports.
- Employee personal information.

Requirements:
- Access must be limited to users with a valid business need.
- Information must be stored in approved business systems.
- External sharing must be limited and checked carefully.
- Email recipients must be verified before sending.
- Files must not be shared publicly.
- External sharing links must be restricted and removed when no longer required.
- Confidential information must not be stored in personal email, personal cloud storage, or unauthorised applications.
- Accidental disclosure must be reported immediately.

## 11. Restricted Information
Restricted information is highly sensitive information requiring stronger protection.

Examples include:
- Identity documents.
- Serious claims documents.
- Legal correspondence.
- Breach investigation records.
- Cyber incident reports.
- Admin credentials.
- Privileged access details.
- Backup recovery credentials.
- Sensitive employee records.
- High-risk supplier security information.

Requirements:
- Access must be strictly limited.
- Access must be approved by the Operations Manager or Managing Director.
- Information must be stored only in approved restricted-access locations.
- Sharing must be limited to authorised recipients.
- Restricted information should not be sent by email unless necessary and appropriate safeguards are used.
- Restricted information must not be downloaded to unmanaged personal devices.
- Access should be reviewed periodically.
- Disposal must be secure and controlled.
- Any suspected exposure must be treated as a security incident.

## 12. Labelling and Marking
Where practical, documents should be labelled according to their classification.

Examples:
`Public`
`Internal`
`Confidential`
`Restricted`

Recommended document footer examples:
`Classification: Internal`
`Classification: Confidential`
`Classification: Restricted`

For SME practicality, not every document requires visible labelling. However, important policies, reports, registers, incident documents, and sensitive templates should include classification labels where useful.

## 13. Storage Requirements
Information must be stored in approved locations based on sensitivity.

| Information Type | Approved Storage |
| Public Information | Website, approved marketing platforms, public document locations. |
| Internal Information | SharePoint, OneDrive, Microsoft 365, approved internal folders. |
| Confidential Information | CRM, SharePoint restricted folders, Xero, approved business systems. |
| Restricted Information | Restricted-access SharePoint folders, approved secure vaults, approved business systems with limited access. |

Users must avoid storing business information in:
- Personal email accounts.
- Personal cloud storage.
- Personal messaging apps.
- Unapproved file-sharing tools.
- Unsecured USB drives.
- Local device folders where better approved storage is available.

## 14. Sharing Requirements
Information must only be shared with authorised recipients.

Sharing requirements include:
- Verify recipients before sending sensitive information.
- Share only the minimum information required.
- Use approved business systems and communication channels.
- Restrict external sharing links.
- Remove access when sharing is no longer required.
- Avoid public links for Confidential or Restricted information.
- Confirm unusual requests before sharing customer, policy, claims, or finance information.
- Report accidental sharing immediately.

Restricted information should only be shared externally when there is a clear business need and approval from the Operations Manager or Managing Director.

## 15. Email Handling
Email is a common source of accidental data disclosure.

Users must:
- Check the recipient before sending customer or policy documents.
- Avoid using personal email accounts for business information.
- Avoid forwarding customer information unnecessarily.
- Use clear subject lines without unnecessary sensitive details.
- Avoid sending identity documents unless required.
- Report incorrect recipient incidents immediately.
- Verify unusual requests for customer or financial information.

## 16. Printing and Physical Documents
Where physical documents are used, they must be protected.

Requirements include:
- Do not leave Confidential or Restricted documents unattended.
- Store sensitive printed documents securely.
- Collect printed documents promptly.
- Avoid printing sensitive information unless necessary.
- Dispose of sensitive documents securely.
- Do not take customer documents outside approved work locations unless required for business purposes.

## 17. Data Disposal
Information must be disposed of securely when no longer required.

Disposal requirements include:
- Delete electronic information from approved systems when no longer required and permitted by retention requirements.
- Use secure deletion where available for sensitive files.
- Shred or securely dispose of printed Confidential or Restricted documents.
- Remove access to shared folders and links when no longer required.
- Do not dispose of customer or policy information in general waste.
- Consider business, legal, regulatory, and insurance retention requirements before deletion.

## 18. Data Retention
HarbourShield should retain information only for legitimate business, legal, regulatory, insurance, or operational reasons.

Retention expectations include:
- Customer and policy records should be retained according to business and legal requirements.
- Incident records should be retained for governance and improvement purposes.
- Unnecessary duplicate copies should be removed where practical.
- Old documents should be archived or deleted according to approved retention decisions.
- Employees must not delete records to hide incidents, errors, or unauthorised activity.

Retention periods should be confirmed by the business owner and external compliance adviser where required.

## 19. Use of Real Data for Testing or Training
Real customer information must not be used for testing, training, demonstrations, or screenshots unless approved.

Requirements include:
- Use dummy or anonymised data where possible.
- Remove names, contact details, policy numbers, identity details, and financial information from examples.
- Do not use screenshots containing real customer data in training material unless approved.
- Do not upload real customer data into unauthorised AI tools, testing tools, or external services.
- Report accidental use of real data in unauthorised locations.

## 20. Data Breach and Incident Reporting
Users must report suspected or confirmed exposure of Confidential or Restricted information immediately.

Examples include:
- Email sent to the wrong person.
- Public sharing link created by mistake.
- Lost laptop containing customer documents.
- Unauthorised access to CRM records.
- Identity document sent to the wrong recipient.
- Supplier reports a breach.
- Customer information found in an unauthorised location.
- Sensitive file uploaded to an unauthorised application.

Reports should be made to the Operations Manager, who will coordinate with the outsourced IT provider, Managing Director, and external compliance adviser where required.

## 21. Roles and Responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Managing Director | Approves this policy and high-risk data handling decisions. |
| Operations Manager | Owns this policy, coordinates data handling guidance, and manages incidents involving sensitive information. |
| System Owners | Classify information within their systems and approve access where required. |

| Employees | Classify, handle, store, share, and report issues involving information appropriately. |
| External Compliance Consultant | Provides advice where privacy, breach notification, or regulatory interpretation is required. |

## 22. Policy Exceptions
Exceptions to this policy must be documented and approved.

Examples include:
- Temporary external sharing of Confidential information.
- Use of an alternative storage location during a system outage.
- Retention of documents longer than normal for legal or claims reasons.
- Temporary use of real data for approved testing or investigation.

Exceptions must include:
- Business reason.
- Data involved.
- Classification level.
- Risk description.
- Compensating controls.
- Approval owner.
- Expiry or review date.
