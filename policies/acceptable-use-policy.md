# Acceptable Use Policy

### Project: Cyber Security Governance Framework for an Australian Insurance Brokerage
### Organisation: HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd
### Document Owner : Operations Manager
### Approved By : Managing Director
### Version : 1.0
### Document Status : Portfolio Project – Fictional Business Scenario 

## 1. Purpose
The purpose of this Acceptable Use Policy is to define how employees, contractors, temporary workers, and authorised third parties must use HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd systems, devices, accounts, internet access, email, cloud platforms, and business information.

HarbourShield handles customer personal information, insurance policy documents, claims information, financial records, business customer information, and internal company documents. Acceptable use requirements help reduce the risk of data loss, unauthorised access, malware infection, business email compromise, fraud, and reputational damage.

This policy is designed for a small-to-medium Australian insurance brokerage using cloud-based systems and outsourced IT support.

## 2. Policy Objectives
The objectives of this policy are to:
1. Define acceptable and unacceptable use of company systems and information.
2. Protect customer, employee, insurer, supplier, and company information.
3. Reduce cyber security risks caused by unsafe user behaviour.
4. Support secure use of email, cloud systems, internet access, and company devices.
5. Reduce the risk of malware, phishing, credential theft, and accidental data exposure.
6. Support accountability for employees and authorised third parties.

## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, temporary workers, and authorised third parties.
- All company-owned laptops, mobile devices, cloud accounts, email accounts, collaboration platforms, and business applications.
- Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, insurance CRM, Xero, WordPress, insurer portals, and other HarbourShield systems.
- Internet access, email communication, file sharing, remote work, and use of customer information.
- All customer, employee, supplier, insurer, policy, claims, financial, and operational information.

## 4. General Acceptable Use Requirements
Users must use HarbourShield systems responsibly, lawfully, and for authorised business purposes.

Users must:
- Use approved systems for business work.
- Protect company and customer information from unauthorised access or disclosure.
- Follow security policies and procedures.
- Use their own assigned accounts and credentials.
- Keep passwords and authentication methods secure.
- Lock devices when unattended.
- Report suspicious emails, unusual activity, lost devices, or suspected incidents promptly.
- Use customer information only for legitimate business purposes.
- Respect confidentiality when handling insurance, claims, policy, and financial information.
- Follow instructions from the Operations Manager and outsourced IT provider during security events.

## 5. Unacceptable Use
Users must not use HarbourShield systems, devices, or information in a way that creates security, legal, operational, or reputational risk.

Users must not:
- Share passwords or authentication codes.
- Use another person’s account without approval.
- Attempt to bypass security controls.
- Disable endpoint protection, device encryption, logging, or security settings.
- Install unauthorised software or browser extensions.
- Store business information in unauthorised personal cloud storage.
- Send customer or company information to personal email accounts.
- Access customer, employee, insurer, supplier, or finance information without a valid business reason.
- Copy, download, or transfer sensitive information without approval.
- Use HarbourShield systems for unlawful, offensive, fraudulent, harmful, or inappropriate activity.
- Use business systems to harass, abuse, discriminate, threaten, or intimidate others.
- Deliberately visit websites known to host malware, illegal content, or harmful material.
- Connect unauthorised devices to company systems or networks.
- Ignore or hide a suspected security incident.

## 6. Email and Communication Use
Email is a key business tool and must be used carefully.

Users must:
- Use HarbourShield-approved email accounts for business communication.
- Check recipients before sending customer, claims, policy, or finance information.
- Treat unexpected attachments, links, and payment requests with caution.
- Verify unusual bank detail change requests using a separate trusted channel.
- Report phishing emails or suspicious messages promptly.
- Avoid forwarding business emails to personal email accounts.
- Avoid sending sensitive information to unauthorised recipients.
- Use professional language in business communications.
- Avoid clicking links or opening attachments from suspicious senders.

Users must not:
- Send customer documents to personal email addresses for convenience.
- Send passwords, MFA codes, or privileged credentials by email.
- Use email to knowingly distribute malware, spam, or harmful links.
- Pretend to be another person or misrepresent HarbourShield.

## 7. Internet Use
Internet access must be used responsibly and mainly for legitimate business purposes.

Acceptable internet use includes:
- Accessing insurer portals.
- Accessing cloud business systems.
- Performing customer, supplier, or insurance-related research.
- Using approved business and productivity tools.
- Accessing training, regulatory, or cyber awareness material.
- Accessing professional information relevant to the user’s role.

Unacceptable internet use includes:
- Visiting websites known to contain malware or illegal content.
- Downloading unauthorised software.
- Accessing inappropriate, offensive, or harmful content.
- Using company systems for excessive personal browsing.
- Using business systems for gambling, piracy, cryptocurrency mining, or unauthorised commercial activity.
- Attempting to bypass web filtering or security controls.

## 8. Cloud Systems and File Sharing
HarbourShield uses cloud systems such as Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, CRM, Xero, and insurer portals.

Users must:
- Store business information in approved systems.
- Use SharePoint, OneDrive, CRM, or other approved repositories for business documents.
- Restrict access to sensitive customer and company information.
- Check file-sharing permissions before sharing documents.
- Avoid unnecessary external sharing.
- Remove external sharing access when no longer needed.
- Avoid creating duplicate copies of sensitive information.
- Report accidental sharing or suspected unauthorised access.

Users must not:
- Store customer information in personal cloud accounts.
- Share confidential or restricted files using unauthorised tools.
- Make sensitive folders publicly accessible.
- Share customer documents with unauthorised third parties.
- Download large volumes of business information without approval.

## 9. Company Devices
Company devices must be used securely.

Users must:
- Keep company laptops and devices secure.
- Lock devices when unattended.
- Use approved authentication methods.
- Allow security updates and patches to be installed.
- Use endpoint protection provided by the business.
- Report lost, stolen, damaged, or compromised devices immediately.
- Avoid leaving devices unattended in public places.
- Avoid sharing company devices with family members, friends, or unauthorised users.

Users must not:
- Disable security software.
- Modify security settings without approval.
- Install unauthorised software.
- Use company devices to store unauthorised personal or illegal material.
- Connect unknown USB drives or removable media without approval.
- Attempt to remove management controls from company devices.

## 10. Personal Devices and Remote Work
Remote work must be performed securely.

Users must:
- Use company-approved devices where available.
- Avoid storing customer information on unmanaged personal devices.
- Use MFA for remote access and cloud systems where supported.
- Protect screens from unauthorised viewing in public places.
- Avoid using public or shared computers to access sensitive business systems.
- Use secure home Wi-Fi where possible.
- Report lost or compromised devices immediately.
- Follow the same security requirements when working remotely as when working in the office.

Users must not:
- Download customer documents to personal devices unless approved.
- Share work devices or business accounts with family members.
- Use public computers to access CRM, Xero, Microsoft 365, or insurer portals.
- Store business passwords in unsecured personal notes, browsers, or documents.

## 11. Software and Applications
Only approved software and applications should be used for HarbourShield work.

Users must:
- Use authorised business applications.
- Request approval before installing new software.
- Keep browser extensions and plugins limited to business-approved use.
- Report suspicious pop-ups, browser redirects, or software behaviour.
- Allow the outsourced IT provider to remove unsafe or unauthorised software.

Users must not:
- Install pirated, cracked, or unlicensed software.
- Install unauthorised remote access tools.
- Install software that creates security or privacy risks.
- Use unauthorised AI, file-sharing, messaging, or automation tools to process customer information.
- Bypass software restrictions or security controls.

## 12. Use of Customer and Insurance Information
Customer and insurance information must be used only for legitimate business purposes.

Users must:
- Access customer records only when required for their role.
- Handle insurance policy, claims, and identity documents carefully.
- Share customer information only with authorised parties.
- Follow approved processes for sending customer documents to insurers.
- Use secure and approved systems to store customer documents.
- Report accidental disclosure or incorrect recipient errors promptly.
- Avoid discussing customer matters in public or insecure locations.

Users must not:
- Access customer records out of curiosity.
- Use customer information for personal reasons.
- Share customer documents with unauthorised people.
- Copy customer information into personal files, personal devices, or unauthorised tools.
- Use real customer information for training, testing, or demonstrations without approval.

## 13. Passwords and Authentication
Users are responsible for protecting their login credentials.

Users must:
- Use strong passwords or passphrases.
- Keep passwords secret.
- Use MFA where required.
- Report suspected password compromise immediately.
- Avoid reusing business passwords for personal accounts.
- Use an approved password manager where provided.

Users must not:
- Share passwords with colleagues, managers, family members, or third parties.
- Write passwords in unsecured locations.
- Send passwords through email, chat, or SMS without approval.
- Approve MFA prompts they did not initiate.
- Allow another person to use their account.

## 14. Social Media and Public Communication
Users must be careful when discussing HarbourShield online or in public.

Users must:
- Avoid sharing confidential business information on social media.
- Avoid sharing customer, insurer, supplier, or employee information publicly.
- Refer media, public, or formal enquiries to authorised management.
- Be professional when representing HarbourShield online.
- Avoid posting screenshots of internal systems, customer documents, or business emails.

Users must not:
- Publicly disclose internal documents, incidents, credentials, customer details, or security information.
- Use HarbourShield branding without approval.
- Make unauthorised public statements on behalf of the company.
- Share images or posts that reveal sensitive information in the background.

## 15. Monitoring and Privacy Notice
HarbourShield may monitor the use of company systems, devices, accounts, email, cloud services, logs, and security alerts where lawful and appropriate for business, security, operational, compliance, or incident investigation purposes.

Monitoring may be used to:
- Detect suspicious activity.
- Investigate security incidents.
- Protect customer and company information.
- Review access to systems.
- Support system maintenance and troubleshooting.
- Confirm compliance with security policies.

Users should not expect personal privacy when using company systems for activities that are subject to lawful business monitoring.

## 16. Incident Reporting
Users must report suspected or confirmed security incidents promptly.

Examples include:
- Suspicious email or phishing message.
- Lost or stolen laptop.
- Accidental email sent to the wrong recipient.
- Suspected password compromise.
- Unexpected MFA prompt.
- Malware or ransomware warning.
- Unusual CRM, Xero, Microsoft 365, or insurer portal activity.
- Unauthorised file sharing.
- Customer information exposure.
- Website compromise.

Incidents should be reported to the Operations Manager or outsourced IT provider as soon as possible.

## 17. Policy Exceptions
Exceptions to this policy must be documented and approved.

Examples of exceptions include:
- Temporary use of a non-standard device.
- Temporary use of an alternative communication channel during system outage.
- Business need to use a third-party application not yet formally approved.
- Temporary access to a restricted system for support purposes.

Exceptions must include:
- Business reason.
- Risk description.
- Compensating controls.
- Approval owner.
- Expiry or review date.

## 18. Roles and Responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Managing Director | Approves this policy and major exceptions where required. |
| Operations Manager | Owns this policy, coordinates communication, and tracks policy issues. |
| Outsourced IT Provider | Supports technical controls, device security, access restrictions, and incident investigation. |
| System Owners | Ensure acceptable use expectations are followed within their systems. |
| Employees | Follow this policy and report suspicious or unsafe activity. |
| Contractors and Third Parties | Use HarbourShield systems and information only as authorised. |

## 19. Non-Compliance
Failure to comply with this policy may result in action appropriate to the situation.

Possible actions include:
- Security reminder or retraining.
- Removal of system access.
- Review of user permissions.
- Management review.
- Contractor or supplier review.
- Disciplinary action where appropriate.
- Legal or regulatory action where required.

Non-compliance that causes or increases security risk must be reported to the Operations Manager.
