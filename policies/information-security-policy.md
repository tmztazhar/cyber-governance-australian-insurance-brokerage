# Information Security Policy
## Project: Cyber Security Governance Framework for an Australian Insurance Brokerage
## Organisation: HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd

### Document Owner : Operations Manager
### Apporoved by : Managing Director
### Version : 1.0
### Document Status : Portfolio Project – Fictional Business Scenario

## 1. Purpose
The purpose of this Information Security Policy is to define the minimum information security expectations for HarbourShield Insurance Brokers Pty Ltd.

HarbourShield handles customer personal information, insurance policy documents, claims information, financial records, business customer information, and internal company documents. This policy supports the protection of confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information used by the business.

This policy is designed for a small-to-medium Australian insurance brokerage that relies on cloud-based systems and outsourced IT support.

## 2. Policy Objectives
The objectives of this policy are to:
1. Protect customer, employee, insurer, supplier, and company information.
2. Reduce the risk of unauthorised access, data loss, fraud, and business disruption.
3. Support practical cyber security governance across the organisation.
4. Define minimum security responsibilities for employees and service providers.
5. Support secure use of cloud systems, devices, email, and business applications.
6. Provide a foundation for related security policies and procedures.

## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, temporary workers, and authorised third parties.
- All business systems used by HarbourShield.
- All company-owned laptops, mobile devices, cloud systems, and collaboration tools.
- All customer, employee, supplier, insurer, policy, claims, financial, and operational information.
- All information stored, processed, transmitted, or accessed through HarbourShield systems.
- Outsourced IT providers and third-party platforms that support HarbourShield’s operations.

## 4. Information Security Principles
HarbourShield’s information security approach is based on the following principles:

| Principle | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Confidentiality | Information must only be accessed by authorised people. |
| Integrity | Information must be accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorised change. |
| Availability | Information and systems must be available when needed for business operations. |
| Least privilege | Users should only have access required for their role. |
| Accountability | System and information asset owners must be clearly identified. |
| Risk-based security | Security controls should be based on business risk and information sensitivity. |
| Shared responsibility | Security is the responsibility of all employees, not only IT. |

## 5. Information Classification
HarbourShield information must be handled according to its sensitivity and business importance.

| Classification | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Information approved for public release. | Website content, public marketing material. |
| Internal | Information intended for internal business use only. | Internal procedures, staff announcements. |
| Confidential | Sensitive business or customer information requiring restricted access. | Customer records, insurance policy documents, supplier contracts. |
| Restricted | Highly sensitive information that could cause serious harm if disclosed. | Identity documents, breach investigation files, privileged admin credentials. |

Employees must only access, share, store, or transmit information according to its classification.

## 6. Acceptable Use of Systems
HarbourShield systems must be used responsibly and for authorised business purposes.

Employees must:
- Use approved business systems for company work.
- Protect customer and company information.
- Avoid using personal email accounts for business communication.
- Avoid storing customer information on unauthorised personal devices or personal cloud accounts.
- Avoid installing unauthorised software on company devices.
- Lock devices when unattended.
- Report suspicious activity or security incidents promptly.

Employees must not:
- Share passwords.
- Attempt to bypass security controls.
- Access information not required for their role.
- Copy customer data to unauthorised locations.
- Use business systems for unlawful, harmful, or inappropriate activities.
- Disable endpoint protection or security settings without approval.

## 7. Access Control
Access to HarbourShield systems must be controlled based on business need.

Access control requirements include:
- Each user must have a unique user account.
- Shared accounts should be avoided unless formally approved.
- Access must be approved by the relevant system owner or Operations Manager.
- Access must follow the principle of least privilege.
- Access must be removed promptly when an employee leaves the business.
- Privileged access must be limited to authorised users only.
- System access should be reviewed at least every six months.
- External IT administrator access should be reviewed periodically.

## 8. Passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication
Passwords and multi-factor authentication are required to reduce the risk of unauthorised access.

Minimum requirements include:
- Users must create strong passwords or passphrases.
- Passwords must not be shared with other users.
- Passwords must not be reused across personal and business accounts.
- Multi-factor authentication must be enabled for Microsoft 365, CRM, Xero, remote access, and other critical systems where supported.
- Privileged accounts must use multi-factor authentication.
- Suspected password compromise must be reported immediately.

Where practical, HarbourShield should use password management tools to reduce unsafe password storage and sharing.

## 9. Email and Phishing Protection
Email is a key business communication tool and a common source of cyber risk.

Employees must:
- Treat unexpected emails, links, attachments, and payment requests with caution.
- Report suspicious emails to the Operations Manager or outsourced IT provider.
- Verify unusual payment or bank detail change requests through a separate trusted channel.
- Avoid sending sensitive customer information to unauthorised recipients.
- Check email recipients before sending customer, claims, or policy documents.
- Avoid opening attachments from unknown or suspicious senders.

The outsourced IT provider should support appropriate email security controls, including spam filtering, malware protection, and mailbox security configuration.

## 10. Device Security
Company devices must be protected from unauthorised access, malware, and data loss.

Device security requirements include:
- Company laptops must use endpoint protection.
- Devices should be encrypted where supported.
- Operating systems and business applications must be kept updated.
- Devices must be locked when unattended.
- Lost or stolen devices must be reported immediately.
- Only authorised software should be installed.
- Staff must avoid using unmanaged personal devices to store customer information.

## 11. Cloud and File Storage
HarbourShield uses cloud systems such as Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, CRM, and Xero.

Cloud storage requirements include:
- Business information should be stored in approved systems.
- Sensitive files should not be stored in personal cloud accounts.
- External sharing should be restricted and reviewed.
- Access to sensitive folders should be limited to authorised staff.
- Customer and policy documents should be stored in approved business repositories.
- Unnecessary duplicate copies of sensitive information should be avoided.
- Cloud permissions should be reviewed periodically.

## 12. Data Handling and Sharing
Employees must handle customer and business information carefully.

Data handling requirements include:
- Sensitive information must only be shared with authorised recipients.
- Customer documents must be sent only through approved communication channels.
- Emails containing sensitive information must be checked carefully before sending.
- Information should only be retained for legitimate business purposes.
- Documents must be disposed of securely when no longer required.
- Staff must avoid discussing sensitive customer matters in public or insecure locations.
- Accidental disclosure must be reported immediately.

## 13. Backup and Recovery
HarbourShield must maintain backups for important business systems and information.

Backup requirements include:
- Critical systems and information must be backed up where supported.
- Backup responsibilities must be agreed with the outsourced IT provider and relevant vendors.
- Backup status should be reviewed periodically.
- Recovery testing should be performed at least annually for critical information.
- Backup arrangements should consider ransomware and accidental deletion scenarios.
- Backup failures must be investigated and remediated.

## 14. Third-Party Security
HarbourShield relies on third-party providers, including cloud platforms, CRM vendors, accounting systems, website hosting, insurer portals, and outsourced IT support.

Third-party security requirements include:
- Critical suppliers should be identified and recorded.
- Suppliers that access or store sensitive information should be reviewed before use.
- Supplier access should be limited to what is required.
- Supplier access should be removed when no longer needed.
- High-risk suppliers should be reviewed at least annually.
- Supplier security incidents must be reported to HarbourShield promptly where contractually required.
- The Managing Director should approve high-risk supplier relationships.

## 15. Incident Reporting
Employees must report suspected or confirmed security incidents as soon as possible.

Examples of incidents include:
- Suspicious emails or phishing attempts.
- Lost or stolen laptops.
- Suspected password compromise.
- Unauthorised access to customer files.
- Accidental email sent to the wrong recipient.
- Malware or ransomware infection.
- Website compromise.
- Supplier breach notification.
- Unusual CRM, Xero, or Microsoft 365 activity.

Incidents should be reported to the Operations Manager, who will coordinate with the outsourced IT provider and Managing Director where required.

## 16. Security Awareness
All employees must understand their role in protecting information.

Security awareness should cover:
- Phishing and social engineering.
- Passwords and multi-factor authentication.
- Secure handling of customer information.
- Business email compromise.
- Reporting incidents quickly.
- Safe use of cloud file sharing.
- Remote work security.
- Avoiding unauthorised software.

Security awareness should be simple, practical, and repeated periodically.

## 17. Compliance and Exceptions
Employees, contractors, and authorised third parties must comply with this policy.

Policy exceptions must be:
1. Documented
2. Supported by a valid business reason.
3. Reviewed by the Operations Manager.
4. Approved by the Managing Director where the risk is medium, high, or critical.
5. Time-limited where practical.

Failure to follow this policy may result in access removal, management action, supplier review, or other appropriate business action.
